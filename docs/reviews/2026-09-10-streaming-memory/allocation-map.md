# The static allocation map: every structure, its size formula, and its lifetime

Produced by nine agents, one per region of `src/spextractor.cpp`, each map then verified against
the source by a second agent: **268 allocation sites, 67 corrections, 38 sites the first pass
missed**. What follows is the 40 largest on dataset D as the mappers sized them, then the region
summaries. Sizes marked CAPACITY are what the allocator holds, not what the program touched --
`PeakSlab` deliberately over-reserves 2.5x and the untouched tail costs address space, not pages.

*Note on the constant:* the mappers were given ~7.7e8 picked MS2 peaks for dataset D; the tile
reader has since measured **1.258e9**, so multiply their per-peak D figures by 1.63.

## What each region's mapper said dominates it

* This region is dominated by one structure: PeakSlab's three peak arrays (tof 4 B + inten 4 B + imq 2 B per peak), which are the whole run's MS2 peaks and nothing else in the region is within two orders of magnitude of them -- 7.7 GB resident on dataset D at 7.7e8 peaks (the 2026-09-06 ledger measured 12.0 GB at 1.256e9 compact-store peaks), and ~19.3 GB of address space because append()'s grow() over-reserves 2.5x from a running peaks-per-frame projection and nothing is ever shrunk; the second tier is TracePrep::order at 4 B per peak of the live cell (128 MB per window, ~3.1 GB across 24 in-flight windows) plus TracePrep::fidx at 3.3 MB per window, and everything else in the region -- every per-frame array of PeakSlab, all five FrozenWin members, seg, frame_live, cnt/cur -- sums to under 2 MB for the whole run and matters only for the counter to be honest. Two lifetimes look longer than necessary: sliceFrames() copies (line 962) are live *while their source is still live* -- a cell's cslab coexists with wslab because release_input() only runs at line 4711 after the whole cell loop, and in the multi-tile resident arm each tile's tile_slabs copy coexists with the entire never-released ms2_by_window store, so the same peaks are charged twice at 10 B each; and the 1.5x over-reserved tail (11.6 GB on D) rides from the first reallocation during LOAD all the way to release_input(), which means the counter MUST record capacity and size separately or it will over-report RSS by 1.5x -- PeakSlab::reserved() at line 888 and the existing `[slab] ... MB reserved but untouched` log line at 3673 are the calibration for that. The instrumentation shape that avoids any atomic in a per-peak loop is uniform across the region: give PeakSlab and TracePrep a private `charged_` byte count plus a settle() that diffs total capacity bytes and atomically adds the delta, call it once at the end of append()/indexRt()/toTof()/sliceFrames()/prepareTracing() and in destructors and move operations (PeakSlab has no user-declared move ops today, so `wslab = std::move(...)` at 4597 and `ts = sliceFrames(...)` at 4532 would double-count without them), and charge FrozenWin once per window after line 4281 -- that is at most one atomic per frame appended and one per cell prepared, against 7.7e8 peaks.
* Lines 100-720 are dominated by one family repeated four times: TraceStore's float span arena (4 B per slot, ~1.1 slots per picked peak by the tool's own 4.4 B/peak calibration at 4270) plus its int16 bin arena (2 B/slot), instantiated as per-cell/band private stores, per-split-chunk stores, one per-window store, and one run-scope MS1 store — so at the window-loop peak roughly n_conc x (6 B x A_w) of arena coexists with 40 B x every Trace record (the larger line: ~630 MB of records per window against ~210 MB of arena), while everything else the region defines (rtAxis ~277 KB, TofAxis::b_by_frame ~270 KB, the per-store frame tables at ~27 KB each) is four to five orders of magnitude smaller and is worth a named permanent line only so it stays out of the unaccounted remainder. Two lifetimes look longer than necessary: ms1_store.inten, ms1_store's frame tables and ms1_traces ride from the MS1_TRACE phase all the way to the last tile's join at 5159-5161 even though only the compacted precursor XICs are read after 4086, and nothing ever shrinks a window arena or a Trace vector — makeSpan's geometric resize at 522 leaves up to 2x slack, absorb() at 344 holds source and destination simultaneously (the tool's own log at 4812 calls this "x2 while absorbed"), and five erase-remove_if filters on `precursors` shrink size while keeping capacity — so the counter must charge CAPACITY at reserve/absorb sites and take its subtracts from the swap-with-empty points (350, 1435, 1436, 4820, 4877, 5159-5161) and a TraceStore destructor, never from inside a per-peak or per-trace loop.
* Two structures dominate this region and both are multi-GB on D: the tile's compact peak store (`tile_slabs`/`wslab`, 10 B per picked peak = 7.7 GB at size and up to 2.5x that in PeakSlab::append's over-reservation) and the emitted-spectra accumulator (`win_out`, ~9 kB x 6-9e5 spectra = 5.5-8.4 GB), which grows monotonically through the window loop and is therefore at its maximum exactly when the last windows are still tracing; on the OpenMS-detector arm `wmap` (20 B/peak) replaces the slab at ~643 MB per window x up to 24 in flight. Three lifetimes look longer than necessary: `wmap` is dead after `detectTraces_` (old 4889) yet lives to the end of the window body through all of scoring; `win_out`'s spectra ride from the first emission of the first window until the tile's `writeBlock`/`all_out` drain, so nothing is ever written incrementally within a tile; and `ms2_by_window` is never released in the resident multi-tile arm (old 4531), so the whole run's slab coexists with every tile's `sliceFrames` copy.
